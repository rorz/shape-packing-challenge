## Method and scope

A variable chooses one complete (x, y, theta) placement for each semicircle. Domains contain the base and signed LLL moves, with both their position and angular components preserved. Common integer translation is removed from the LLL moves by fixing piece 0's position gauge. The final local-correction model additionally allows piece 0's one-unit local moves. Neither construction fixes the enclosing center. The first four nontranslation directions with nonzero position motion are basis indices 7, 12, 16, 23. Their largest position components are 1, 9, 9, 26 micro units respectively. Each state receives angular offsets -2, -1, 0, 1, 2 micro radians, with duplicates removed.

Every pair-table entry uses the scorer's 4096-point Shapely/GEOS polygon intersection, comparing directly to 1e-6. A pair is omitted only when the rectangles enclosing its possible disk centers have Euclidean separation greater than 2. There are no Taylor area constraints, static chord corrections, fixed-center containment domains, or hardcoded flat-angle relations.

An overlap-feasible assignment is scored with the fast free-center MEC. If it exceeds the target, actual diameter endpoints and analytic farthest boundary points supply point triangles. A triangle's minimum circle is a lower bound on any enclosing circle, independent of every other shape and of its center. A violating triangle yields a no-good on at most three complete placement choices. A 2e-10 numerical guard prevents using near-threshold arithmetic as a small conflict certificate; ambiguous cases block only the complete assignment. Final promotion requires the reference validator and then the public checker.

These are finite-domain experiments. An exhausted model rules out its saved placement choices only. The floating-point triangle guard is not a formal interval-arithmetic proof. Time limits do not prove infeasibility. Angle domains reject unstable round/wrap aliases explicitly rather than silently testing a different geometry.

The total measured main-model time is about 230 seconds. The explicit domains can be reused without table regeneration.

Enlarging that Minkowski sum would be a different finite model, potentially useful, but the present results say nothing about its feasibility. A lazy exact-pair conflict learner could avoid eagerly evaluating its much larger table product. No further search was launched in this task.

## Tighter local loss bound

The global deficit is wasteful for short arcs near a contact. Let h=pi/4095 and epsilon=1-cos(h/2). Every lost chord point has radial coordinate r between cos(h/2) and 1. If such a point belongs to the other semicircle, its outer unit-arc point lies inside that other's disk expanded to radius 1+epsilon and its flat halfplane expanded by epsilon. Let m be the angular measure of this expanded intersection along the first semicircle's arc. Its lost area is at most 0.5*sin(h/2)^2*m. Apply this bound to both shapes and cap each by its global semicircle deficit. This is a set inclusion and annular sector bound; it does not approximate overlap curvature or hold a correction constant.

The native helper intersects the own-arc, expanded-disk, and expanded-halfplane angle intervals. It enlarges the expansion/measure conservatively and retains the same 1e-9 arithmetic allowance. `local-loss-band-audit.json` again has zero disagreements on all 713,323 retained exact GEOS decisions. GEOS ambiguity falls from 139,850 to 15,782. Additional tests compare the actual native true-area minus GEOS polygon area against the loss bound in random overlaps, near-tangent arcs, and nearly opposed flat wedges. Twelve focused tests pass.

The first wide run remains on its original global band. The next wider-angle model will use the new local bound.
